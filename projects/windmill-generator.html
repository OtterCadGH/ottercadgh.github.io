A printed vertical-axis turbine that tells you when it's spinning wrong

A wind-powered generator with an AC-to-DC conversion stage and an analog indicator circuit that lights "too slow," "too fast," and a lamp whose brightness tracks speed. The lab supplied a generator and a stock propeller; I designed and printed a helical vertical-axis turbine and a tripod stand for it instead, then characterized the system at three wind speeds.

OutcomeWorking turbine and indicator system, demonstrated live on a custom-printed vertical-axis rotor, documented with data at three wind speeds
My roleSolo: turbine and stand CAD and printing, rectifier, comparator and gate logic, Multisim model, data collection
ConstraintsDiscrete components only (resistors, diodes, comparators, transistors, LEDs); provided generator; three-week lab window; extra credit only for electrical features, so the turbine was for me
SkillsAnalog circuit design, rectification and filtering, comparator thresholds, Multisim, experimental characterization, turbine CAD and FDM printing

The spec

The generator is a permanent-magnet machine, so its output is AC with a voltage that rises with shaft speed. The assignment required converting that to DC and building an indicator system with three behaviors: a light that comes on when the turbine is not spinning fast enough, a light that comes on when it is spinning too fast, and a light whose intensity increases continuously with speed. Everything had to be built from discrete parts, modeled first in Multisim, and characterized with data at three wind speeds.

The rectified generator voltage is the speed signal. Comparators measure it against reference levels derived from a bench 12 V supply, which only provides the comparison voltages and does not power the indicator circuit. The comparator outputs feed simple logic gates so that exactly one indication is lit at a time: too slow, in range, or too fast, with the brightness lamp driven from the rectified bus directly so it tracks speed continuously.
