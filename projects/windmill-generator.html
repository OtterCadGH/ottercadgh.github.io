A printed vertical-axis turbine that tells you when it's spinning wrong

A wind-powered generator with an AC-to-DC conversion stage and an analog indicator circuit that lights "too slow," "too fast," and a lamp whose brightness tracks speed. The lab supplied a generator and a stock propeller; I designed and printed a helical vertical-axis turbine and a tripod stand for it instead.

OutcomeWorking turbine and indicator system, demonstrated live on a custom-printed vertical-axis rotor
My roleSolo: turbine and stand CAD and printing, rectifier, comparator thresholds, Multisim model, data collection
ConstraintsDiscrete components only (resistors, diodes, comparators, transistors, LEDs); provided generator; three-week lab window; extra credit only for electrical features, so the turbine was for me
SkillsAnalog circuit design, rectification and filtering, LM339 comparators, Multisim, experimental characterization, turbine CAD and FDM printing

The spec

The generator is a permanent-magnet machine, so its output is AC with a voltage that rises with shaft speed. The assignment asked for that to be converted to DC and turned into an indicator system with three behaviors: a light for spinning too slowly, a light for spinning too fast, and a light whose brightness rises with speed. Everything had to be built from discrete parts and modeled first in Multisim.

The rectified voltage, V_motor, is the speed signal: faster rotor, higher DC. A full-bridge rectifier (four 1N4002s, carried over from an earlier lab) and a 10 uF cap turn the AC into that signal. Two channels of an LM339 compare V_motor against two thresholds, each set with a 10 kOhm pot: red comes on under speed, yellow comes on over speed. The green indicator skips the comparators entirely and hangs off V_motor through a 330 Ohm resistor, so its brightness just follows speed. Calibration happened at the fan: spin at the slowest acceptable speed and trim POT1 until red just turns off, spin at the fastest and trim POT2 until yellow just turns on.

Technical details

GeneratorProvided permanent-magnet generator, mounted in the hub of a custom rotor
RotorTwo-blade helical vertical-axis turbine and tripod stand, designed and FDM printed by me
ConversionFull-bridge rectifier, four 1N4002 diodes, with a 10 uF smoothing cap; the DC output V_motor is the speed signal
IndicatorsLM339 quad comparator, two channels used: red under-speed LED against V_low, yellow over-speed LED against V_high, both thresholds set by 10 kOhm pots; green LED driven straight from V_motor through 330 Ohm, brightness tracks speed
SupplyBench supply for the LM339 and threshold pots; the chip needed at least 7 V to behave with this signal range
ModelingMultisim before build

What I would fix

Two things cost me time on the bench. The green indicator was supposed to run through a transistor for a wider brightness range, and I never got that stage biased right, so the LED runs straight off V_motor and only lights above about 2 V. And the indicator circuit needed at least 7 V of supply; I fed it 5 V for a long while and chased phantom problems in the circuit around the LM339 before checking what the chip actually wanted. Budget the supply voltages first, then debug.
