Seven months of building the things that test the things

A co-op on an electrosurgical controller program in medical device R&D. The two biggest builds have their own pages: the connector lifetime test rig and the pneumatic lamination press. This page covers the rest of the job.

The thermal redesign

The controller had components running hotter than IEC 60601 allows. I instrumented the unit with 16 thermocouples, logged everything in Python, and used the maps to drive a cooling redesign that brought component temperatures down 5 degrees C to compliant levels. The same data made the case for the change in review, which mattered as much as the fix.

The ablation fixture, 3.5x stronger

A 3D printed ablation test fixture kept snapping its locating pin. The failure was print orientation: FDM layer lines ran perpendicular to the bending load and acted as a shear plane. I split the pin into a separate keyed body so the base prints upright and the pin prints with its layers along the load path, then quantified the fix with a cantilever test before release. Bending strength improved about 3.5x, and the fixture was released as a new revision through PLM change control with 15+ cross-functional signatures.

The print farm

I also ran the team's in-house additive manufacturing: seven FDM and SLA printers (Fortus, Bambu Lab, Asiga), tuning print parameters and materials so parts came out strong enough to test with and fast enough to keep programs moving.

The two big builds

The connector cycler ran 7,500 verified plug/unplug cycles per receptacle and caught an undocumented vendor spring substitution before formal testing. The lamination press bonds the controller's front assembly at 125 lb for 10 s with no electronics in the control path. Both pages have photos, video, and full technical detail.
