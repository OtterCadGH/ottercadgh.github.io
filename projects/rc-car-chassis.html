A printed chassis around parts nobody else wanted

A team RC car built on a fixed budget. My part was the chassis: a single 3D-printed tub designed in SolidWorks to carry a drivetrain, suspension, and steering that my teammates salvaged from broken RC cars, with the electronics bay, servo mount, shock tower, and bulkheads all in one print.

OutcomeA running car on a chassis printed from my model, with donor suspension and drivetrain bolted straight to it
My roleChassis design: packaging, mounting geometry, print orientation. Teammates: drivetrain, electronics, salvage and assembly
ConstraintsFixed team budget; parts from cannibalized broken cars with unknown geometry; must print on a hobby printer
SkillsSolidWorks, design for FDM, packaging around existing hardware, team integration

Designing around salvage

The interesting constraint was that nothing bolting to the chassis was ours. Suspension arms, hubs, shocks, the steering linkage, and the rear drivetrain all came from donor cars my teammates pulled apart, so the chassis had to match hole patterns and pivot geometry that we measured off broken parts rather than pulled from a drawing.

That pushed me toward one monolithic tub instead of a plate-and-bulkhead stack: fewer interfaces to get wrong, and every mount referenced to the same print. The front carries a raised shock tower on four posts and a pocket for the steering servo; the rear bulkhead is bored for the donor drivetrain and takes the wing mounts. Side ribs stiffen the long open bay that holds the ESC, receiver, and battery.

Print orientation was part of the design. The tub prints upright on its floor so the long walls are continuous in the direction they bend, and the tower posts are sized to print without support.

Technical details

ChassisMonolithic FDM tub, SolidWorks; front shock tower on four posts, steering servo pocket, ribbed sidewalls with cover mounts, rear bulkhead with drivetrain bore
MaterialPLA for the main tub, XTCF-20 carbon-fiber PETG for the loaded parts; printed upright on the floor for wall continuity
WheelbaseAbout 15 in
SalvageSuspension arms, hubs, shocks, steering, rear motor and gearbox from broken donor cars (teammates)
ElectronicsESC, receiver, steering servo, LiPo in the bay; printed rear wing
Team splitWill: chassis design and print. Teammates: drivetrain, salvage, electronics, assembly
