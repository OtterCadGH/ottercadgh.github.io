Designing a phototherapy device for newborns

My current internship. I lead the physical design of a neonatal phototherapy device, from concept through ten prototypes to a tested working unit: LEDs, optics, thermal management, internal packaging, and the enclosure, against clinical and regulatory requirements.

OutcomeWorking prototype, validated against the market leader: intensive-level irradiance of 30 uW/cm2/nm or more across 99% of the treatment target
My rolePhysical design lead: component selection, optics, thermal, packaging, enclosure, plus the test rigs to qualify all of it
ConstraintsClinical and regulatory requirements; ongoing commercial program, so specifics stay at resume level here
SkillsOptics for illumination, thermal design, test rig design, GD&T tooling, radiometric measurement

Setting the target, then beating it

The program started with a benchmark: I mapped a leading competitor device with seven-point thermocouple measurements, irradiance readings across its operating modes, and power draw analysis, so our own targets were numbers rather than opinions. The prototype that came out of the program was then validated the same way, with a bili radiometer against the market-leading device: AAP intensive-level irradiance, 30 uW/cm2/nm or more, across 99% of the treatment target.

The lens

Illumination uniformity is the hard optical problem in phototherapy. I designed a freeform lens that reached 99.6% illumination uniformity, against 94.2% for a leading competitor lens, and built the workflow to iterate it in house: printed lens prototypes in a day instead of outside optics vendor timelines. The lens design runs through an in-house computational lens synthesizer I created; the program itself is proprietary, so that is all I can say about it here.

Printed optics had to earn their place before machined acrylic vendors got involved, so I built a blackbox transmission rig and measured the clear-coated SLA lenses at 93% white and 88% blue transmission.

The unglamorous parts that ship

I built FDM printed assembly jigs with GD&T callouts through three revisions that halved adhesive strip application time while holding repeatable bezel alignment. Weekly design reviews to engineering leadership across an 11 week program kept all of it honest.
