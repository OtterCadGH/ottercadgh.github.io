The UT Tower on a desk, lit like the real one

A desk-scale model of the UT Austin Tower, modeled in SolidWorks from the original architectural blueprints and printed in white resin, with an ESP32 inside that reads the university's tower-lighting page and lights the model to match: white on an ordinary night, burnt orange after a win.

The source

Most tower models online are eyeballed from photos. I worked from the original "Main Building and Library Extension" drawings instead, the north elevation of the tower at 1/8 in to the foot, with every tier elevation called out down the left margin. The setbacks, the observation-deck colonnade, the clock face, and the window rhythm in the model all come from that sheet.

The lighting

The real tower changes its lighting for football wins, graduation, and other events, and the university publishes the current status on a web page. An ESP32 in the base polls that page over Wi-Fi, parses the configuration, and drives the model's LEDs to match; it has no schedule of its own, it shows whatever the university publishes. The walls are printed thin enough that the window cutouts glow when lit from inside, which is what makes the lighting states readable instead of just a bright base.
